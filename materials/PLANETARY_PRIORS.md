# Planetary priors

Planetary environments are long-duration material experiments. Their surviving
structures supply starting hypotheses for field-guided computational matter:

- iron–nickel meteorites preserve slow diffusion and phase separation;
- native copper and silver preserve mineral dendrites;
- vacuum, radiation, stress, and thermal cycling expose whisker growth in
  spacecraft electronics;
- microgravity suppresses buoyant convection and increases the relative role
  of diffusion and interfacial fields;
- giant-planet interiors place matter in conductive hydrogen, high pressure,
  strong heat, and planetary magnetic fields.

## Iridium and niobium on Saturn

Saturn has no solid surface. Two regimes give different first predictions:

| Regime | Conditions | Ir–Nb implication |
|---|---|---|
| One-bar cloud level | `134 K`; atmosphere approximately `96.3% H2` and `3.25% He` | Iridium and niobium remain solid. A dilute molecular gas supplies negligible ionic material transport, so the first developmental-growth prediction is a null. |
| Deep conductive interior | Interior models place metallic hydrogen near `2e11 Pa` and about `8000 K` | The host becomes conductive and both metals enter high-pressure, high-temperature, hydrogen-reactive phase space. Iridium trihydride has been synthesized to `125 GPa`; niobium forms multiple hydrides. Saturn-specific phase stability and mobility remain the quantities to calculate. |
